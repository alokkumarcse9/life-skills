# Prevention of Sexual Harassment

### 1. What kinds of behaviour cause sexual harassment?

**Unwelcome Physical Contact:** This includes touching, stroking someone's hair, brushing against someone's body, pinching, patting, grabbing, kissing, or more serious acts like molestation.

**Offensive Verbal Comments:** Using foul language, telling "dirty" jokes, or making comments about a person's body or sexual life.

**Inappropriate Visual Behavior:** This includes leering or staring at someone's body, making suggestive gestures, or displaying sexually explicit pictures and screen savers.

**Unwanted Advances and Messages:** Making repeated, unwelcome requests for dates or sending sexually offensive content through emails, texts, or phone calls.

**Gender-Based Discrimination:** Acts like gender-based insults, stereotyping, or denying equal career opportunities based solely on a person's sex.

### 2. What would you do if you face or witness any incident or repeated incidents of such behaviour?

**Tell the Harasser to Stop:** Directly inform the individual that their behaviour is unwelcome and ask them to stop immediately.

**Keep a Detailed Record:** Document every incident, including the dates, times, locations, what happened, your response, and any potential witnesses.

**Report to the ICC:** If the behaviour continues after you have asked them to stop, report the incident in writing to a member of the Internal Complaints Committee (ICC).

**Who Can File:** A complaint can be filed by the aggrieved person, a co-worker, relative, friend, or anyone who has knowledge of the incident.

**Police Action:** If the situation warrants it, you also have the right to file a complaint with the police regarding assault or any other applicable offence.
